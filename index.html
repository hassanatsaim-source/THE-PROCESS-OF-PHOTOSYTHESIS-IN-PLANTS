The Mechanism of Photosynthesis

1. Light-Dependent Reactions (Thylakoids)

Occurring inside the chloroplast grana, chlorophyll absorbs sunlight photon energy. This energy splits water molecules ($H_2O$) absorbed through the roots into oxygen gas ($O_2$), protons, and electrons. Oxygen is released as a valuable byproduct into the atmosphere.

2. Light-Independent Reactions / Calvin Cycle (Stroma)

Using energy carriers generated in the first stage (ATP and NADPH), the plant fixes carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) absorbed from the air via stomata. Through enzymatic reactions, carbon atoms are assembled into glucose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$), providing nourishment and energy storage for plant growth.

3. Environmental Limiting Factors

  • Sunlight Intensity: Directly powers light-dependent reactions up to saturation point.
  • Water ($H_2O$): Essential electron donor; water scarcity triggers stomata closure, choking $CO_2$ intake.
  • Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$): The raw carbon source for glucose synthesis in the Calvin cycle.
  • Temperature: Influences enzyme activity (optimum typically 20°C–30°C; extreme heat denatures enzymes).
